*Creative Direction for the Homepage.*
*This document is the authority for all visual implementation decisions.*
*No code. No layout. No components. Pure direction.*
*Date: 2026-06-13*

---

## CONCEPT EVALUATION — REJECTED DIRECTIONS

Before naming what this brand is, it is necessary to name what it is not.
Every rejected concept below was considered in full and discarded with cause.

---

### Rejected: Premium Fashion House

The most tempting direction. Extreme restraint. Near-empty pages. Photography doing all the work. Celine, The Row, Bottega Veneta.

**Why it fails here:** Fashion house visual language is debt drawn against brand recognition. The visitor who arrives at a near-empty Celine page already knows Celine. They find the silence beautiful because they understand what the silence is protecting. Doğrular Seramik is building recognition, not trading on it. A visitor who encounters an almost-blank page for an unknown brand does not think "confident luxury." They think "broken website." Fashion house restraint requires the brand equity that makes the restraint legible. That equity does not yet exist.

---

### Rejected: Minimal Japanese Precision

Muji. Kenya Hara. The philosophy of reduction to essence. Wabi-sabi. The thing itself, unadorned.

**Why it fails here:** Japanese minimalism has a specific cultural visual register: very soft, very warm, often paper-white or unbleached linen. It is inseparable from its warm material palette. Our color strategy is dark-anchored, near-black in the authority sections. These two directions are fundamentally incompatible. Applying Japanese precision to a dark palette does not produce Japanese minimalism — it produces an approximation that is less convincing than the original and less intentional than a direction built for this brand. Additionally, Japanese precision is a IMPORTED aesthetic for a Turkish brand. The authenticity problem is significant.

---

### Rejected: Scandinavian Restraint

HAY. &Tradition. Fredericia. Warm, accessible, honest materials. The democratic premium.

**Why it fails here:** Scandinavian visual language requires warm neutral surfaces — linen, cream, natural white. DESIGN.md and D-001 explicitly reject this palette as the AI default for the entire ceramic and craft category. Every brand attempting "accessible premium" in the material space reaches for Scandinavian restraint. The result is categorical invisibility. The brand must look different from its competitors before it can claim to be different. Scandinavian restraint guarantees it will not.

---

### Rejected: Museum Experience

MoMA. Fondation Louis Vuitton. Objects in isolation. White walls. Extreme negative space. The curatorial voice.

**Why it fails here partially:** The museum experience treats its objects as FINISHED. A finished sculpture, a finished painting. Ceramic surfaces are NOT finished objects — they are materials that are realized in use. The museum aesthetic unintentionally communicates "look but do not touch, do not install, do not specify." For an architectural material brand, this is the wrong relationship with the product. The museum direction also creates an accessibility problem for B2C homeowners, who feel neither invited nor represented by institutional whiteness.

**What survives from the museum direction:** The deliberate pacing. The sense that each section earns its own attention before the next arrives. The refusal to rush. These BEHAVIORS are absorbed into the synthesis; the full aesthetic direction is not.

---

### Rejected: Editorial Luxury

Dezeen. Wallpaper*. Architectural Digest. Strong typographic hierarchies. Large type. Photography as editorial subject.

**Why it fails as a primary direction:** Editorial luxury is a communication approach, not a brand approach. A magazine can be editorial luxury because its product IS the editorial experience. For Doğrular Seramik, the product is a ceramic surface. The editorial direction risks making the website feel like content about ceramic surfaces rather than a ceramic surfaces brand. The difference is critical. Editorial luxury also has a well-documented problem with conversions — visitors read, appreciate, and leave, without committing to an action.

**What survives from editorial luxury:** The typographic confidence. The asymmetric grid. The willingness to let a page breathe. These compositional tools are absorbed into the synthesis.

---

### Rejected: Pure Material Storytelling

Laminam's technical poetry. Corian's close-up obsession. The brand as technical authority.

**Why it fails alone:** Material storytelling, when it is the only mode, is beautiful for professionals and invisible to consumers. The homeowner does not arrive looking for a technical education. The professional does not need to be taught about ceramic manufacturing to make a specification decision. Material storytelling is most effective as a supporting layer WITHIN a brand direction, not as the brand direction itself. When material presentation is the primary visual language, the brand becomes a manufacturer rather than a brand.

**What survives from material storytelling:** The photography mode that reveals material depth. The close-up moments in the Craft section. The oblique-angle shots that communicate three-dimensionality. These are absorbed.

---

## THE SYNTHESIZED DIRECTION: "ARCHITECTURAL GRAVITY"

What the six rejected directions could not achieve individually, the synthesized direction achieves through deliberate combination:

**The authority of Italian material culture** (surfaces as architecture, not decoration) combined with **the compositional intelligence of editorial luxury** (asymmetric grids, typographic confidence) combined with **the deliberate pacing of museum experience** (each moment earns attention) combined with **the material honesty of material storytelling** (photography that reveals rather than flatters).

The name: **ARCHITECTURAL GRAVITY**.

The principle: Doğrular Seramik surfaces are presented with the same weight and authority that architecture gives to its permanent materials. Not decoration. Not finish. Not styling. Structure. The surfaces define spaces; they do not merely occupy them. The visual direction communicates this at every scroll depth, in every composition decision, in every typographic choice.

## 1. ART DIRECTION

The homepage is an architectural proposition, not a product catalog.

Every visual decision passes through a single filter: **"Would a Herzog & de Meuron monograph use this?"** Not because this is an architecture firm, but because architecture at this level treats materials with the same seriousness that Doğrular Seramik intends for its surfaces. When Herzog & de Meuron shows stone, the photography reveals the stone's internal logic. When they show concrete, the image reveals how light moves across its texture over the day. That is the standard of material presentation this direction establishes.

The art direction has two registers, held in tension:

**The authority register** (dark sections): Near-black ground. Photography that is lit architecturally — precise, directional, revealing. Typography that speaks at scale, without decoration. This is where the brand makes its claims.

**The clarity register** (light sections): Near-white ground. Photography that is color-accurate, not color-beautiful. Systematic presentation that serves evaluation rather than seduction. This is where the brand keeps its promises.

The tension between these two registers — dark authority and light clarity — is not a problem to be solved. It is the brand's visual argument: we are beautiful AND we are exact. We are aspirational AND we are specifiable. The two registers prove both halves of the promise.

## 2. COMPOSITION PHILOSOPHY

Every composition is asymmetric by intention. Symmetry is not allowed as a default — it must be chosen for a specific reason, stated explicitly. There are no symmetrical compositions on this homepage.

The governing compositional rule: **the most important element always commands the most space, not the largest size**. A single surface photograph with 60% of the section's area dedicated to it, surrounded by 40% near-black, communicates more authority than three photographs occupying the same total area.

The compositional hierarchy within each section operates from a single dominant element. One element is primary. Everything else is subordinate. In sections with photography, the photography is primary. The typography is never the dominant element in sections that contain photography. This is a discipline: typography's role is to name, orient, and deepen — not to compete with the image for primacy.

The 12-column grid is the structural skeleton. It is never used symmetrically. Within any given section, the grid allocation is always biased — one side heavier, one side lighter. The asymmetry is not random: it follows the directionality of the reading eye (left-to-right in European languages, top-down in vertical weight hierarchy).

Spatial relationships between elements follow an internal proportion system. If the headline is 80px, the space between the headline and the subtext is not 24px (a CSS default) — it is derived from the display size. The proportional system creates visual coherence across sections without making the page feel templated.

**What compositional approaches are banned:**
- Equal-weight left/right splits where neither side dominates
- Three-column grids where all columns receive equal visual weight
- Elements centered horizontally without asymmetric supporting elements
- Photography cropped to fill a container without consideration of the image's internal composition

## 3. SCALE PHILOSOPHY

Scale is radical and intentional. The difference between the largest and smallest typographic elements on any given section is extreme — minimum 4:1 ratio, typically 6:1 or higher.

Display type at the headline level is genuinely large. Not "large for a website" — large. At 1440px viewport, the primary headline occupies approximately 10–14% of the viewport height as a typographic element. This is architectural scale: the kind of scale you find in environmental graphics, not in interface design. The scale communicates that what is being said is worth saying at that scale.

Photographs in the full-bleed hero occupy 100% of the viewport. There is no border, no margin, no container that reduces the photography to anything less than the full viewport. The photography IS the section, not a component within it.

In the collections grid, the scale differential between the featured tile (4/7 of the grid width) and the standard tiles (2/7 each) creates visual hierarchy without color differentiation. The visitor's eye is directed by proportion, not by color emphasis.

In the brand statement section, the text is the scale event. Without photography, the type must carry the visual weight at display scale. The body copy that follows is dramatically smaller — approximately 20% of the display size. This vertical scale contrast creates emphasis that photography would otherwise provide.

**Scale philosophy summary:** Always choose the scale that communicates the importance of the element. Never default to a "comfortable" size. Discomfort in scale is a tool — it prevents the eye from passively scanning and demands active engagement.

## 4. WHITE-SPACE PHILOSOPHY

White space — including its dark equivalent, the near-black negative space in dark sections — is the page's primary luxury signal. The philosophical foundation: **what is not shown is as important as what is**.

The calculation for white space begins with what feels like too much, then doubles it. If the section padding feels "generous" in a layout draft, it is not enough yet. Luxury material brands at the caliber this homepage aspires to — Laminam, Poliform, Rimadesio — treat vertical section height as a resource to be given away, not hoarded.

Specific white-space commitments:
- Vertical section padding: minimum 120px above and below content on desktop. Not 80px. Not 96px. 120px as the floor.
- Display elements occupy no more than 60% of their section's total area. The remaining 40% is dedicated to breathing room.
- The collections grid has a minimum 80px gutter between the grid and the section edge at 1440px viewport.
- In the brand statement section, the text block occupies approximately 50% of the horizontal viewport width, centered. The 25% on each side is unoccupied.
- The featured project left column uses vertical white space as a compositional element — not every portion of the column is filled with content.

**What white space is not:** Placeholder emptiness awaiting future content. Leftover space after elements are placed. A compromise between competing elements.

**What white space is:** The editorial decision to say something in a specific amount of space, and to give that statement the room it requires to land.

## 5. TYPOGRAPHY HIERARCHY

The typographic system has three and only three levels. A fourth level does not exist on this homepage.

**Level 1 — The Brand Voice (Display)**
The largest text on the page. Used for the homepage headline, the brand statement, and featured collection names in the collections grid. Exclusively for moments of primary communication.

Character: Geometric but not cold. The capitals must have genuine personality — not friendly approachability but architectural confidence. The letterforms should feel like they were designed with the same rigor that produces precision components. Consider the difference between Inter's neutral geometric circles and Söhne Breit's assertive geometric character, or between ABC Diatype and PP Neue Montreal Display. This typeface is the brand's voice — it must sound like something.

Setting: Tight tracking (−0.03 to −0.05em). The headline is never ragged-right when it breaks — it is composed so that the break is a decision, not an accident. Line height is compressed (0.95 to 1.05) for multi-line headlines. The type sits on its own terms, not as a component of a layout system.

Sizing: The headline in the hero section is set using `clamp()` with a minimum at mobile of approximately 2.5rem and a maximum at desktop of 6–7rem. The brand statement text is set larger — approximately 3.5–4rem at desktop — because it is the sole occupant of its section. These are starting values, not locked values. The final setting must be judged in the browser against the actual photography.

**Level 2 — The Brand's Knowledge (Body)**
All descriptive content: project descriptions, craft section body, space labels, collection category text. This level does the informational work of the page.

Character: The body type must complement the display type without competing. If the display type is strongly geometric, the body type can be the same family at lighter weight — or a humanist sans that provides warmth against the geometric display. Two typefaces maximum in this system. Mixing is permitted but must be justified.

Setting: Leading 1.6–1.7 (generous — this is reading text, not display text). Maximum line length 65ch. Size 16–18px at desktop. On dark sections, the body text is 90–95% white (not pure white — pure white on dark backgrounds creates irradiation that makes the text harder to read at sustained reading length). On light sections, the body text is 80–90% ink (not pure black — the softening maintains material luxury; pure black body text reads as utility, not craft).

**Level 3 — The Brand's Reference System (Label)**
Collection categories. Project locations and years. Certification marks. Section orientation text. Micro-information that marks and orients without speaking.

Character: The same typeface as the body, at 11–12px, in uppercase, with tracked letter-spacing (0.10–0.15em). Used sparingly — appearing in approximately 3–4 instances across the full homepage. Not every section has a label. The label's value derives from its rarity.

**Typography rule:** No fourth typographic level exists on this homepage. If a design decision requires a fourth level, it is not a typographic level — it is a design problem that requires a compositional solution instead.

**Font selection principle:** Inter is categorically excluded. The choice must be made from typefaces with genuine personality in their capital letterforms. The selection is made after the color system is finalized, because the type must be tested against the actual backgrounds at the actual scale in the actual color context. What reads perfectly in a specimen may flatten when set white on near-black at 100px.

## 6. PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

Photography is the primary medium. The visual direction lives or dies on its quality.

Three photographic modes, each with specific direction:

**Mode 1: Architectural Photography (Hero, Featured Project)**

These are complete environments. A fully realized space in which the ceramic surface is the material that makes the space possible — not the decoration applied to an already-complete space. The distinction is critical: a photograph in which the ceramic tiles are incidental to an already-beautiful room is not an architectural photograph for this brand. It is a lifestyle photograph. The correct photograph is one in which the ceramic surface IS the architecture — where you cannot imagine the space without the surface.

Lighting: Natural or architectural. Never studio-lit architectural photography. The lighting must feel like the actual light conditions of the space — morning light through eastern windows, diffuse northern light in a gallery, the warm late-afternoon light of a residential terrace. The light must reveal the surface's behavior under the conditions it will actually experience.

Color: Neutral color temperature (5500–6500K). Not warm. Not cinematic. Accurate. An architect must be able to assess material color from this photograph. Color grading must preserve material truth.

Human presence: Optional. If present, soft-focus and peripheral — the human is in the space, but the space is not organized around the human. Stock-photograph human presence is categorically excluded.

**Mode 2: Editorial Photography (Collections Grid)**

These are surface-in-context photographs at an angle and distance that reveals the material's character. Not the full room. Not the isolated tile. The surface as it meets light at an oblique angle — the moment that reveals grain, depth, texture variation. This is the photography that makes the visitor understand that this surface has an interior life, not just a surface.

Composition: The photograph is composed to fill the collection tile, with the surface filling approximately 70% of the frame and the architectural context providing 30%. The context establishes scale and application without overwhelming the material presentation.

**Mode 3: Material Photography (Craft Section)**

Close-up. Abstract. The surface at a scale that reveals what the eye cannot see from installation distance. The grain of the porcelain. The way the glaze sits. The micro-texture that creates the surface's light behavior. These photographs are technically beautiful but not decorative — they prove the quality of manufacturing at a level of detail that specifications cannot.

This photography does not try to be beautiful. It tries to be accurate. Its beauty is the beauty of precision.

**Photography prohibitions:** No styled props (vases, books, organic materials) placed on ceramic surfaces to make them feel "lifestyle." No artificial warm color grading that flatters the image at the expense of material accuracy. No photography with heavy post-processing vignettes, film grain filters, or vintage color shifts. No photography where the ceramic surface is secondary to the furniture or decor in the space.

## 7. MATERIAL PRESENTATION PHILOSOPHY

A ceramic surface is three-dimensional. A screen is two-dimensional. The visual direction must bridge this gap without misrepresenting the material.

**The three-dimensionality problem** is solved by photography that includes oblique angles. A surface photographed directly head-on reveals color and pattern. A surface photographed at 15–30 degrees from oblique reveals color, pattern, texture depth, and light behavior. The oblique photograph is always preferable for material presentation.

**The color accuracy imperative** overrides the beauty imperative in all material presentation contexts. The most flattering photograph of a surface is not necessarily the most useful. An architect specifying floor materials for a €2M residence needs to know what color the tiles actually are under the light conditions of the space. That requirement supersedes the desire to make the photograph beautiful.

**Scale references** are present in all architectural and editorial photography. A recognizable architectural element (a standard door height, a human hand, a skirting board thickness) gives the viewer an implicit scale reference that allows them to understand the tile format. Scale references are compositional elements, not afterthoughts.

**Material context before material isolation:** The presentation sequence in the collections grid moves from context (the surface in a space) to detail (the surface at close range). Never the reverse. The visitor must first understand what the material CREATES before they understand what it IS. The sequence is aspirational (context) then technical (detail), never technical-first.

## 8. MOTION PHILOSOPHY

The governing test for every motion decision: **"Would this motion be appropriate in an architectural documentary film?"**

Not a music video. Not a product reveal. Not a SaaS demo reel. An architectural documentary — Herzog & de Meuron: No. 1, a documentary about the firm. The motion language of that context: slow, controlled, purposeful. Cameras move to reveal, not to impress. Cuts are deliberate. The pacing respects the complexity of what is being shown.

This is the motion calibration for the homepage.

**Deceleration as a value:** Every animation in the page decelerates — it enters at higher speed and arrives at zero velocity. Never an animation that arrives at constant speed (linear easing). Never an animation that overshoots its target (spring-physics overshoot). The deceleration communicates weight. A lightweight object decelerates quickly; a heavy object decelerates slowly. Ceramic surfaces, architecture, gravity — these are heavy. The animations must feel it.

**Duration:** 700ms minimum for entry animations. 1100ms for the most important entry (the hero headline). These are long by digital standards. Digital convention has compressed animations to 150–300ms in the name of efficiency. Efficiency is not the value being communicated here. Substance is. An animation that lasts 900ms says "this matters" in the same way that a long pause before speaking says "listen."

**Stillness as the default state:** After every animation completes, the page is still. Elements do not micro-animate perpetually. There is no ambient motion, no breathing elements, no endless parallax. The page is still, and the photography is still, and the visitor looks at what is in front of them. Motion is an event, not a state.

**The GSAP ScrollTrigger exception:** The pinned featured project section is the one place where the page is in continuous motion — responsive, scroll-controlled, never animated automatically. This is not ambient motion; it is visitor-controlled narrative. The visitor chooses the pace. The page responds. This is the highest-trust motion gesture on the page: yielding control to the visitor to tell their own story at their own pace.

**Hover states:** Express intent, do not announce themselves. A collection tile hover acknowledges the cursor with a 3% scale increase (within overflow: hidden) over 600ms. The motion says "I see you" without saying "look at me!" The distinction is everything.

## 9. INTERACTION PHILOSOPHY

The homepage's interaction model follows the emotional arc: the visitor begins as a viewer and, through the scroll journey, earns the right to become a decision-maker.

**The viewer phase (Sections 01–02):** Interaction is near-zero. The hero has one button. The brand statement has no interactive elements. The page does not ask anything of the visitor here. It presents. The visitor's only action is to scroll — the page's most passive gesture. This patience is unusual in digital design and is itself a luxury signal. Brands that do not need to grab your attention every second are brands that know what they have.

**The explorer phase (Sections 03–05):** Interaction becomes invited. Tiles respond to hover. The featured project responds to scroll. The space tiles extend a visible invitation — their hover states communicate "this wants to be explored." The visitor is no longer passive; they are actively navigating. The interaction is still responsive rather than demanding — the page waits for the cursor before it responds.

**The decision phase (Sections 06–07):** Interaction is direct. Ghost buttons. Text links. Clear affordances. The visitor who has arrived at this depth has self-qualified through the scroll journey. The interaction at this point must be frictionless — nothing that adds a step between intent and action.

**Touch consideration:** Every interactive element has a minimum touch target of 48px. On mobile, hover states are replaced with focus states triggered on first tap. The collections grid becomes a vertical scroll, not a horizontal one. No swipe-based carousels replace vertical scroll — swipe carousels add interaction complexity that serves discovery only when the visitor is willing to commit the gesture. Vertical scroll is the default mobile discovery behavior and should not be replaced.

**Cursor behavior:** The default cursor at all times. No custom cursors, no custom cursor follower animations, no cursor trail effects. Custom cursors are a 2019-era agency portfolio pattern that has become a negative signal for serious product brands.

## 10. SCROLL PHILOSOPHY

The homepage is a single, continuous scroll. It is not a series of discrete pages, panels, or tabs. The visitor moves through time as much as through space.

**Scroll pacing is non-uniform by design.** The page slows down in proportion to the importance of what the visitor is encountering. The hero passes in approximately 0.5–1 seconds of scroll. The brand statement in 1–2 seconds. The collections grid requires 4–6 seconds of genuine attention. The featured project can occupy 15–30 seconds of controlled scroll. The space explorer 4–6 seconds. The craft section 2–3 seconds. The dual CTA 1–2 seconds.

This non-uniform pacing is achieved through section height, not through scroll-speed manipulation. The page does not control how fast the visitor scrolls (except in the pinned section). It controls how much there is to scroll. A pinned section where 300vh of scroll distance reveals a single project gives the visitor the time and space to genuinely engage. A section with only 70vh of scroll is brief and direct.

**The GSAP pin** in the featured project section is the page's one moment of maximum engagement. The visitor is not being manipulated — they are being invited into the most immersive moment the page offers. The pin says: "Take your time. Look at this. This is what we are capable of." The experience of the pin is that the visitor feels held — gently, not forcibly. They can scroll through quickly or slowly. The page responds at the pace they choose.

**What scroll is never:** A mechanism for delay. A vehicle for reveal animations that gate content. A series of forced pace-changes that remove the visitor's sense of agency. Scroll hijacking beyond the one pinned section is explicitly prohibited.

**Mobile scroll:** On mobile, the experience is a standard vertical scroll from top to bottom. No horizontal scroll sections, no pinned panels, no scroll-linked animations except fade-up reveals. The mobile scroll is generous and fast. The visitor who scrolls quickly on mobile should still encounter every section. Content is never hidden behind slow-reveal mechanics that can be accidentally skipped.

## 11. LUXURY PERCEPTION STRATEGY

Luxury is not communicated through the presence of expensive-feeling elements. It is communicated through the consistent ABSENCE of everything that is not necessary.

**Absence as signal:**
The homepage contains no testimonials, no awards strip, no "as seen in" logo wall, no social proof badge, no email signup, no live chat widget, no cookie consent banner (managed separately), no pop-up modal for newsletter, no floating contact button. Each of these elements is present on the websites of brands that are anxious about conversion. Anxiety is not luxury. The brand that removes all of these elements communicates something more powerful than any of them: confidence.

**Specificity as luxury:**
The brand statement says something specific about surfaces. Not "we create beautiful surfaces" but a statement that only someone who genuinely understands ceramic materials could make. The craft section gives ISO certification numbers, not just "certified." The featured project names the architect, the location, the project year — not "a prestigious residential project in a major city." Specificity is the language of the brand that has something real to say. Vagueness is the language of the brand that does not.

**Restraint of color as luxury:**
Three colors constitute the entire homepage: near-black, near-white, and one accent. The accent appears in fewer than five instances across the entire page. Each instance of the accent color is a considered decision, not a default. When a color is used rarely enough that every instance is noticed, the color means something. When a color is used everywhere, it means nothing.

**Photography quality as the primary luxury signal:**
No brand strategy, typographic system, or compositional philosophy can rescue mediocre photography. Conversely, exceptional photography communicates the brand's quality before any other element loads. The single most important production decision for this brand is the quality of the architectural photography brief. The photographer must understand that they are photographing architecture, not products. The ceramics are not the subject; the spaces they create are.

**Time as luxury:**
The page is slow. Not slow to load — it must load instantly. Slow to reveal. Slow to complete. The visitor who experiences the homepage as a 30-second scroll journey has received a different communication than the visitor who bounces after 3 seconds. The page is designed for the visitor who stays — and communicates to those who stay that staying was worth it.

## 12. EMOTIONAL JOURNEY

The visual system must deliver specific emotional states. Each section's visual character is calibrated to its emotional function.

**STRUCK (Hero) — Visual character:** Maximum contrast. Full-bleed photography of maximum quality. The display headline enters from the left via clip-path — as if the architecture itself is revealing the brand's voice. No other motion. After entry, complete stillness. The visitor is stopped by what they see before they have processed what they think about it. This is the correct order — sensation precedes cognition.

**INTRIGUED (Brand Statement) — Visual character:** The photography is gone. The dark near-black field remains. Large type, white, occupies approximately 50% of the section width. The type lines appear one at a time, fading up from below, 100ms stagger. Then: stillness. The visitor is reading something specific and considered. They lean in. The absence of photography in this section is itself a statement — the brand is confident enough to ask for attention without a visual reward.

**INTERESTED (Collections) — Visual character:** The page opens to light. The near-white ground arrives and the collections grid fills it. Six tiles, asymmetric, staggered entry at 80ms intervals. The featured tile arrives first — it is larger, it commands the eye. The visitor's eye moves through the grid actively, scanning. The visual character here is clarity and invitation. Nothing obstructs the photography. Nothing competes with the tiles. The visitor evaluates.

**COVETOUS (Featured Project) — Visual character:** Darkness returns. A single project, immersive. The left column is still; the right breathes through images at the visitor's own scroll pace. The photography in this section is the most significant architectural photography in the site — a complete space, multiple angles, genuine scale. The visitor is held here. Dwell time is the KPI of this section. The visual character is cinematic — the visitor is inside something, not looking at something from outside.

**SEEN (Space Explorer) — Visual character:** Light returns. Six tiles, each a complete room. The visual character is recognition — the visitor finds their context. The tiles are composed to show the SPACE as the hero, not the ceramic surface. The ceramic surface IS the space in these images — the floor, the wall, the surface that defines the room's character. The visitor's eye finds the tile that corresponds to their project.

**REASSURED (Craft) — Visual character:** Dark again, but the visual character is entirely different from the hero's dark. No photography dominates. The visual field is text and numbers — specific, legible, dense. The numbers count up on entry (data point count-up animation, the only section where motion is data-driven). The visual restraint communicates: this section exists to inform, not to seduce. That restraint IS the reassurance.

**CLEAR (Dual CTA) — Visual character:** Two panels. Near-black, slightly lighter than the primary dark (creating a subliminal sense that the page is "coming forward" at the end). Ghost buttons. Minimal typography. The visitor has been on a complete journey. They do not need to be persuaded. They need to be directed. The visual clarity of two panels with two choices is the direction.

## 13. VISUAL HIERARCHY

Visual hierarchy operates at three nested scales.

**Page-level hierarchy:** The dark sections hold authority over the light sections. The visitor's overall experience is of alternating density — dark weight, light clarity, dark weight, light clarity, dark weight again. The dark sections make the brand's claims; the light sections allow those claims to be evaluated. The page-level hierarchy communicates: this brand speaks with authority, then shows you that the authority is warranted.

**Section-level hierarchy (universal rule across all sections):**
1. Photography (primary — always the dominant element where present)
2. Display headline (primary where no photography)
3. Body text (secondary — deepens the primary element)
4. Label/meta text (tertiary — marks and orients)
5. CTA elements (quaternary — present only in designated sections)

No section violates this order. The CTA button is never larger than the section's primary element. The label is never given more visual weight than the body text. The hierarchy is enforced, not aspirational.

**Element-level hierarchy:** Within any single element (a collection tile, a project image, a body text block), there is one primary sub-element and everything else is subordinate. A collection tile's primary sub-element is the photography. The collection name is subordinate. The category label is tertiary. Three levels within each element; no more.

**Visual hierarchy prohibition:** No element may compete with the section's primary element for visual attention. If an element threatens to compete, it is reduced, removed, or repositioned to a subordinate role. Competition between elements is a design failure, not a design decision.

## 14. ANIMATION HIERARCHY

Animations are organized in three tiers. Tiers are never active simultaneously.

**Tier 1 — Cinematic (GSAP ScrollTrigger pin): Featured Project only**

The only Tier 1 animation on the homepage. The pinned scroll-driven project narrative. ScrollTrigger `scrub: 1` with a calibrated ratio so that 1dvh of scroll advances the image crossfade by approximately 20%. The left column is `position: sticky; top: 0`. The right column's images crossfade via opacity timelines scrubbed to scroll position.

This is the homepage's single most complex motion moment. It is the correct place for that complexity: the section that must hold the visitor's attention longest, that makes the most significant credibility claim, that proves the brand's capability at full architectural scale.

Nothing else on the page reaches this complexity tier.

**Tier 2 — Choreography (Section entry animation): All other sections**

Section entry animations triggered by IntersectionObserver. The governing principle: each section has ONE orchestrated entry sequence, and that sequence is specific to the section's emotional function.

- Hero: Clip-path reveal on headline (left-to-right, 1.1s, custom cubic-bezier deceleration). Image pre-loaded, no entry animation on the image — it is simply there when the page loads.
- Brand Statement: Text lines fade up, 100ms stagger between lines, 700ms per line.
- Collections: Scale entry (0.96 → 1.0) with 80ms stagger between tiles.
- Space Explorer: Same as collections, 60ms stagger.
- Craft: Simple opacity fade (0 → 1, 500ms). Data points count up from zero on entry.
- Dual CTA: Two panels enter with 150ms stagger, scale 0.98 → 1.0.

Each section has a DIFFERENT entry character. No two sections share the same animation pattern. The variety prevents scroll fatigue and ensures each section's entry communicates its own character.

**Tier 3 — Micro-interaction (CSS transitions): Always available**

Hover states on collection tiles (image scale 1.03, accent underline on name, 600ms).
Hover states on space tiles (image scale 1.04, label reveal, 500ms).
Hover states on CTA buttons (background/color shifts, 200ms).
Navigation link hover states (underline reveal, 200ms).

Tier 3 animations are never disabled. They are never motion-reduced (the scale transforms in Tier 3 are sufficiently subtle that they do not trigger vestibular issues for reduced-motion users — but reduced-motion users should still see the color/opacity transitions).

**Cross-tier rule:** Tier 1 is active only when the visitor is scrolling through the pinned section. Before the pin engages, Tier 2 handles section entries. After the pin releases, Tier 2 handles subsequent section entries. Tier 3 is always active. No two tiers operate simultaneously on the same element.

**`prefers-reduced-motion` implementation:** All Tier 1 and Tier 2 animations collapse to opacity-only (0 → 1, 300ms, no easing required). No transforms, no scale, no clip-path. The page remains fully legible and navigable. The GSAP pin behavior is removed entirely for reduced-motion users — the pinned section becomes a standard vertical scroll of images.

## 15. WHAT MUST NEVER APPEAR VISUALLY

These are absolute prohibitions for this homepage. They are not preferences. They are rules.

**Typography on photography:** Text never overlaid on photography as a primary label. Collection names, section headlines, project names — none of these sit on the photographic surface. They occupy a dedicated compositional zone above, below, or beside the image. The photography is a primary element and is not used as a background surface for other elements.

**Gradient backgrounds:** The dark sections are solid near-black. The light sections are solid near-white. No gradient fills in any section. No mesh gradients. No aurora gradients. No gradient used as atmosphere or decoration. The one functional transition is the 60px edge-to-edge fade that marks the boundary between dark and light sections — this is architectural, not decorative.

**Warm-tinted photography:** All photography is processed at neutral to cool color temperature (5500–6500K). Warm color grading (under 5000K) is prohibited because it distorts material color and makes architectural photography feel like lifestyle photography. The distinction is critical for a brand whose professional audience must make material specification decisions.

**The feature icon grid:** Three (or four, or five) cards, each with an icon, a short headline, and 2–3 lines of description body text, arranged in equal columns. This is the most widely detected AI design pattern in existence. It does not appear in any form on this homepage. If the brand's benefits require visual enumeration, the compositional solution is not a card grid — it is found in the Craft section's specific data points and in the Collections section's material range.

**Shadow-box hover lift:** Collection and space tiles do not lift toward the viewer with an increased box-shadow on hover. This pattern is borrowed from e-commerce product cards and signals the wrong category to the professional audience. The hover state is a contained scale transform within overflow: hidden. The tile does not change its relationship to the page plane.

**Decorative icon sets:** If an icon appears on the homepage, it is functional. Navigation arrow, form submission indicator, certification mark. No icon grids. No feature icons. No decorative icon treatments.

**Warm-cream-and-brass palette in any section:** No section of the homepage uses the warm-cream / sand / linen background with brass or ochre accent that constitutes the most-detected AI default for the ceramic and home goods category. This palette has achieved full saturation in the category and communicates genericness rather than brand identity. The dark-anchored color strategy (D-001) is the inoculation against this pattern.

**Testimonials with attribution photographs:** Human faces in circular crops attached to quoted text do not appear on this homepage. The social proof mechanism on this homepage is the named, credited architectural project — the highest-trust form of social proof available to a material brand. Testimonials are a weaker signal and belong on a different page.

**Infinite scroll in the collections preview:** The six-tile collections grid has a defined end. It is not a loading grid. The visitor does not scroll further within the collections section to see more — they click "Tüm Koleksiyonlar" to go to the Collections Index. The curation of six tiles is the editorial statement.

**Custom cursor effects:** The browser's default cursor at all times. No cursor-tracking animated elements. No magnetic cursor effects. No custom cursor shapes or icons. These patterns peaked in 2019–2021 and now communicate "agency portfolio from that era" rather than premium material brand.

**Looping video backgrounds:** No section uses a video as a background element. If video appears on this homepage in a future iteration, it is featured content playing in a dedicated editorial frame — never a looping, dimmed, blurred atmospheric background.

**Animated text that loops perpetually:** No element on the homepage is in continuous motion outside of active user interaction or the scroll-controlled featured project pin. No typewriter loops. No scrolling marquees. No counting numbers that reset and count again. Ambient motion is prohibited.

**Parallax on typographic or UI elements:** Background images may have maximum 5% parallax shift over 100dvh. Text elements never parallax. Navigation never parallaxes. CTA buttons never parallax. Parallaxing UI elements create accessibility problems and communicate visual busyness that undercuts the gravity of the brand direction.

**Any visual element that has not earned its presence:** This is the final prohibition and the one that governs all others. Every element on the homepage exists because its removal would make the page less capable of communicating the brand's position, building the visitor's trust, or directing the visitor toward action. If an element does not do one of these three things, it is not on the page.
