## GOVERNING INSIGHT

The homepage exists at the intersection of two irreconcilable audiences — the professional who evaluates materials and the aspirant who imagines spaces — and must serve both without either feeling secondary. The architecture resolves this not through compromise but through SEQUENCE. Both audiences are addressed in full, in the order their psychology demands.

The professional needs: brand credibility → material range → proof of execution → specification path.
The aspirant needs: emotional world → aesthetic desire → personal relevance → sensory next step.

These two journeys overlap more than they diverge. The difference is emphasis, not content. A project case study serves as credibility proof for the professional AND as aspirational imagery for the homeowner. A space-organized navigation serves the homeowner's mental model AND gives the professional a secondary application filter. The homepage is architected so both journeys are valid simultaneously.

**The single governing principle:** Make the visitor want something before showing them how to get it.

## 1. COMPLETE SCROLL JOURNEY

The visitor enters the page at 0px scroll. What they encounter at every scroll threshold:

### 0px — Hero (100dvh)
The full viewport is occupied by a single architectural image. The navigation is transparent. A display headline of 2 lines maximum. A single CTA button below it. Nothing else competes. The visitor is stopped.

### 100dvh — Brand Statement (~65–75vh)
A seamless continuation of the dark world. The photography recedes. Large type fills the field. The brand's point of view, in 2–4 sentences of display-scale body text. No subheadings. No bullets. One voice, one idea. The scroll has carried the visitor from WORLD into BELIEF.

### ~165–175vh — Collections Preview (~110–120vh)
The dark world releases. A clean light section opens. Six collections presented as an asymmetric editorial grid. Each has a name, a material category, and a hero image. This section is systematic but not clinical. The visitor is now in EVALUATION MODE — scanning, comparing, considering.

### ~285–295vh — Featured Project (200–280vh of scroll distance — pinned)
The light gives way to darkness again. A single project occupies the full width. On desktop: the left column is pinned. As the visitor scrolls, 4–6 project photographs advance on the right. The visitor is slowed down, held, made to look carefully. The brand proves what it can do, in real architecture, at real scale.

### ~485–575vh (post-pin) — Space Explorer (~90–100vh)
Light returns. Six spaces presented as a discovery grid. The Space Explorer re-engages visitors who felt the previous sections were too professional or too abstract. This is the recognition moment: "This is for MY space."

### ~575–675vh — Craft & Trust (~80–90vh)
The final dark act begins. Numbers, specifics, heritage. No marketing language. The visitor who is still scrolling at this depth is a serious prospect — they want proof of operational credibility before committing to a next step.

### ~655–765vh — Dual CTA (~60–70vh)
The page closes. Two panels, two audiences, two clear next steps. The visitor who has made this journey is not sold TO — they are DIRECTED. The brand has done its work. The CTA is a courtesy, not a pitch.

## 2. SECTION ORDER

### Section 01 — HERO
Position: First. Non-negotiable.

### Section 02 — BRAND STATEMENT
Position: Second, immediately after hero.

**Why this order:** The hero creates visual desire. The brand statement must follow immediately while that desire is active and before the visitor's rational mind starts filtering. If the brand statement came after collections, the visitor would already be in evaluation mode (rational) and the philosophical positioning would feel unnecessary. It must arrive while the visitor is still in emotional reception — wide open from the hero.

**Challenge considered:** Could the brand statement be integrated INTO the hero? Rejected. A hero that contains both a headline AND a manifesto paragraph is a hero that does two things. Heroes that do two things do neither well. The headline is the hook. The manifesto is the follow-through. They need different scroll moments.

### Section 03 — COLLECTIONS PREVIEW
Position: Third.

**Why this order:** The visitor has been given the brand's world (01) and the brand's beliefs (02). Only NOW are they ready to see what the brand MAKES. If collections appeared first, they would be evaluated without context — as commodity tiles, not as brand objects. The psychological sequence is: accept the world → understand the worldview → evaluate the output.

### Section 04 — FEATURED PROJECT
Position: Fourth, after collections.

**Why this order:** The collections establish WHAT the brand makes. The featured project proves WHAT IT BECOMES. This is the critical transition from "material I might like" to "outcome I deeply want." The visitor sees a collection in editorial form (section 03), then sees that collection at full architectural scale in a real project (section 04). This is the most powerful sequence on the page.

**Why not earlier:** A project case study before the collections would establish credibility for a brand the visitor hasn't evaluated yet. Credibility only has value once the visitor has something to be credulous ABOUT. The collection context makes the project proof meaningful.

### Section 05 — SPACE EXPLORER
Position: Fifth, after the featured project.

**Why this order:** The featured project is the homepage's highest-stakes moment — cinematic, immersive, scroll-controlled. After it, the visitor needs a change of pace. The Space Explorer is lighter, more inviting, more browser-friendly. It gives the visitor agency again after the deliberate pace of the pinned project section. It also serves as a second chance for homeowners who may have found the featured project "too architectural."

**Challenge considered:** Should Space Explorer come BEFORE the Featured Project? Rejected. The Space Explorer is an invitation to go deeper. Its invitation is more powerful after the visitor has seen what "going deeper" looks like (the featured project). The emotional sequence: desire the material → see what it creates → now find YOUR context.

### Section 06 — CRAFT & TRUST
Position: Sixth.

**Why this order:** Trust must be EARNED, not demanded. A brand that opens with "trust us, we have 25 years of experience" is insecure. A brand that shows its world, its beliefs, its range, its executed work, its contextual relevance — and THEN tells you about its operational credibility — is secure. By section 06, the visitor already WANTS to trust the brand. The craft section gives them permission.

**Challenge considered:** Could this be section 02, right after the hero? Rejected firmly. Trust-building content (certifications, manufacturing heritage, years of experience) is rational content. It belongs in the rational phase of the page, after the emotional foundation is laid. Beginning a luxury brand's homepage with credentials is a defensive posture that undercuts the aspiration.

### Section 07 — DUAL CTA
Position: Last. Non-negotiable.

**Why last:** The conversion moment belongs at the moment of maximum readiness. A visitor who has completed sections 01–06 has been given everything they need to make a decision. The CTA at the end is not a closing argument — it is a door held open.

## 4. EMOTIONAL PROGRESSION

The visitor's emotional state at the END of each section — not during, but after:

| Section | Emotional State | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Hero | **STRUCK** | Visual arrest. A non-verbal "this is something." The emotion precedes rational processing. |
| 02 Brand Statement | **INTRIGUED** | The emotion deepens and becomes intellectual. "This brand thinks differently." Desire becomes curiosity. |
| 03 Collections | **INTERESTED** | Curiosity becomes active evaluation. The visitor is now in the mode of "is there something here for me?" |
| 04 Featured Project | **COVETOUS** | Evaluation transforms into desire. The visitor wants to be in that space. They are no longer evaluating — they are wanting. |
| 05 Space Explorer | **SEEN** | The visitor recognizes their own context. "This is for someone like me." Recognition activates belonging. |
| 06 Craft & Trust | **REASSURED** | The emotional and rational finally align. The visitor who wanted the product now has permission to trust it. Desire becomes confidence. |
| 07 Dual CTA | **CLEAR** | Clarity is an emotion. The visitor knows what to do. The journey has removed ambiguity. |

**The arc:** STRUCK → INTRIGUED → INTERESTED → COVETOUS → SEEN → REASSURED → CLEAR

This is not a linear escalation — it is a psychological wave. Emotional intensity peaks at COVETOUS (section 04), then softens to SEEN (section 05), settles to REASSURED (section 06), and resolves to CLEAR (section 07). The descent from peak emotion to resolution is intentional: it mirrors the natural arc of consideration for a high-value purchase.

## 5. USER PSYCHOLOGY PROGRESSION

The cognitive/motivational state at each section — distinct from emotion:

| Section | Psychological State | The Visitor's Internal Question |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Hero | **Orientation** | "Am I in the right place?" |
| 02 Brand Statement | **Positioning** | "What makes this brand different?" |
| 03 Collections | **Evaluation** | "Do they make something I could use?" |
| 04 Featured Project | **Social Proof Processing** | "Have serious people trusted this brand?" |
| 05 Space Explorer | **Self-Identification** | "Is this brand relevant to MY situation?" |
| 06 Craft & Trust | **Risk Assessment** | "Can I trust this brand to deliver?" |
| 07 Dual CTA | **Decision Facilitation** | "What do I do with this?" |

**Why this sequence works:** The seven questions must be answered in order. A visitor cannot meaningfully ask "do they make something I could use?" (evaluation) without first establishing "what makes this brand different?" (positioning). A visitor cannot ask "can I trust them to deliver?" (risk assessment) before "have serious people trusted this brand?" (social proof) has been answered. The sequence is not arbitrary — it follows the logical dependency chain of a considered purchase decision.

## 6. CONVERSION PROGRESSION

Conversion is not a single moment. It is a progressive commitment that builds through the page.

### Micro-Conversion Moments (Depth of Engagement)

**Moment 1 — Hero CTA (~0–30 seconds on page)**
Visitor type: HOT (referred, brand-aware, ready to act)
Action: "Örnek İste" CTA below the headline
Expected click rate: 5–10% of all visitors
Implication: This visitor needs no more of the journey. The button must be visible and frictionless.

**Moment 2 — Collections Grid (~60–120 seconds)**
Visitor type: WARM (evaluating range)
Action: Click on a specific collection tile → Collection Detail page
Expected click rate: 20–35% of visitors reaching this section
Implication: This is the highest-volume engagement action on the page. The collection grid must be irresistibly browsable.

**Moment 3 — "Tüm Koleksiyonlar →" link (end of Collections section)**
Visitor type: WARM professional (wanting to see the full catalog)
Action: Collections Index page
Expected click rate: 8–15% of visitors reaching the collections section
Implication: This visitor is not yet ready to sample but is deeply evaluating. The Collections Index is their destination.

**Moment 4 — Featured Project engagement (scroll depth)**
Visitor type: WARM professional or aspirational consumer
Action: Scrolling through the pinned project (not a click, but dwell time signal)
Implication: High dwell time in this section is the single best signal of purchase intent. Analytics must track scroll completion % within this section.

**Moment 5 — "Tüm Projeler →" link (end of Featured Project section)**
Visitor type: WARM architect (wants to assess full portfolio breadth)
Action: Projects Index page
Expected click rate: 10–18% of visitors who complete the featured project section

**Moment 6 — Space Explorer click (~150–240 seconds)**
Visitor type: WARM consumer (has a specific space in mind)
Action: Click on a Space tile → Space Detail page
Expected click rate: 15–25% of visitors reaching the Space Explorer
Implication: This visitor converts to qualified lead primarily through the showroom path after visiting the space page.

**Moment 7 — Dual CTA (~4–8 minutes on page)**
Visitor type: HOT (completed the full journey)
Action: Catalog download OR showroom inquiry
Expected click rate from dual CTA section: 30–45%
Implication: This is the highest-intent conversion point per visitor. The visitor who reaches here has self-qualified through the full journey. The CTA friction must be minimal.

### The Conversion Funnel

```
100% arrive at page
  80% see hero (20% bounce immediately)
  60% reach collections (filter: did the hero earn their scroll?)
  45% reach featured project (filter: did the collections earn their scroll?)
  30% reach space explorer (filter: did the project earn their scroll?)
  20% reach craft section (filter: is the visitor genuinely interested?)
  12% reach dual CTA (filter: is the visitor a serious prospect?)
  4–6% of all visitors convert (sample request, catalog download, showroom)
```

These are TARGETS, not guarantees. The 4–6% conversion target is ambitious for a new luxury brand homepage. The quality of photography is the single biggest variable.

## 7. VISUAL RHYTHM

Visual rhythm governs how the visitor's eye MOVES through the page. Monotony is the enemy — identical section layouts create scroll blindness.

| Section | Layout Family | Orientation | Density | Dominant Element |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Hero | Full-bleed single image | Horizontal | Low (one element) | Photography |
| 02 Brand Statement | Centered text | Vertical | Very low (text only) | Typography |
| 03 Collections | Asymmetric grid | Diagonal (grid rhythm) | Medium (6 tiles) | Photography + Name |
| 04 Featured Project | Horizontal split (pinned L / scroll R) | Horizontal | Medium | Photography sequence |
| 05 Space Explorer | Even 6-tile grid | Grid | Medium-high | Photography + Label |
| 06 Craft & Trust | Data columns + text | Horizontal split | Low-medium | Text + Numbers |
| 07 Dual CTA | Two equal panels | Horizontal split | Low | Text + CTA button |

**Rhythm pattern across sections:**
IMMERSIVE → TEXT → GRID → SPLIT-CINEMATIC → GRID → COLUMNS → SPLIT

No two consecutive sections use the same layout family.
No section is symmetrically identical to another section.
Full-bleed occupies section 01 only. After that, the grid and split patterns create structure.

**The visual arc:** The page moves from pure immersion (hero fills everything) through increasing STRUCTURE (grid, split, columns) to deliberate SIMPLICITY (dual CTA — two panels, nothing else). The structure increases as the visitor becomes more rational, then simplifies again for the decision moment.

## 8. DARK/LIGHT RHYTHM

Color mode governs the emotional WEIGHT of each section. Dark = authority, gravity, aspiration. Light = clarity, discovery, rationality.

```
Section 01 — HERO             ████ DARK
Section 02 — BRAND STATEMENT  ████ DARK      (continuous with 01)
             ─── transition ───────────────
Section 03 — COLLECTIONS      ░░░░ LIGHT
             ─── transition ───────────────
Section 04 — FEATURED PROJECT ████ DARK
             ─── transition ───────────────
Section 05 — SPACE EXPLORER   ░░░░ LIGHT
             ─── transition ───────────────
Section 06 — CRAFT & TRUST    ████ DARK      )
             ─── no break ───────────────── ) unified
Section 07 — DUAL CTA         ████ DARK      ) closing act
```

**Pattern: DARK-DARK | LIGHT | DARK | LIGHT | DARK-DARK**

### Why this specific rhythm

**Sections 01–02 (dark-dark):** The brand world must be sustained long enough to establish emotional investment. A single-viewport hero is not enough for a luxury material brand. The manifesto section DEEPENS the dark world. Breaking to light after just the hero would interrupt the brand's gravity before it has landed.

**Section 03 (light):** Product evaluation requires a clear, neutral environment. The same way an architect examines material samples under a light source, the collections must be presented in a light context for fair evaluation. The light transition after the dark manifesto is also a visual RELIEF — the visitor surfaces from the brand's world into clean discovery space.

**Section 04 (dark):** The featured project returns to darkness because it is a GRAVITY moment. Architecture photography at its most powerful reads on dark backgrounds — the spaces are lit within the image, and dark surroundings make those lit spaces glow. The return to dark also signals to the visitor: "pay attention, something important is happening."

**Section 05 (light):** After the intensity of the featured project, the space explorer is a breath. Light backgrounds feel inviting and accessible. The visitor who was impressed (and perhaps slightly intimidated) by the flagship project is now shown: "here are the everyday spaces we serve." The light makes it approachable.

**Sections 06–07 (dark-dark, unified closing):** The page closes in darkness. This is deliberate. The closing act of a luxury brand's homepage should feel weighty, authoritative, final. Light closings feel unresolved. The craft section's specifics and the dual CTA's invitation both carry more gravity on a dark ground. The visitor completes the journey in the brand's world — the same world they entered in section 01.

### Transition strategy between sections
- DARK→LIGHT: A full-bleed image at the boundary bleeds out of the dark section into the light — the photography itself is the bridge.
- LIGHT→DARK: The background deepens at the section boundary — a gradient from --ds-surface-100 to --ds-ink-900 over 60px.

## 9. MOTION RHYTHM

Each section has a specific motion character. Motion is not applied uniformly — each section's motion serves its emotional function.

| Section | Motion Character | Specific Motion Events | After Entry: State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Hero | ACTIVE — entry and pulse | Title: clip-path left-to-right reveal (1.1s, ease-material). Image: very subtle parallax (5% at 100dvh). CTA: breath-pulse after 1.5s to draw eye. | STILL — photography holds without fidgeting |
| 02 Brand Statement | CALM — single entry | Text: fade-up stagger per line (700ms, 100ms stagger). Nothing else. | COMPLETELY STILL — the text must be read, not watched |
| 03 Collections | RHYTHMIC — staggered entry + hover | Grid tiles: scale 0.96→1.0 + opacity (700ms, 80ms stagger). Hover: image scale 1.03 within card (600ms). | STILL between hovers — only reacts to user intent |
| 04 Featured Project | CINEMATIC — scroll-driven | GSAP ScrollTrigger pin. Left column: stationary. Right: images cross-dissolve on scroll position. Image entry: scale 1.04→1.0 per image. | CONTROLLED BY VISITOR SCROLL SPEED |
| 05 Space Explorer | INVITING — hover-led | Tiles: scale 0.97→1.0 on entry (600ms, 60ms stagger). Hover: image scale 1.04 + label reveal (500ms). | RESPONSIVE — tiles wait for cursor invitation |
| 06 Craft & Trust | FACTUAL — minimal | Section entry: opacity fade (500ms). Data points: count-up animation on entry (numbers only). Nothing else. | STILL — technical credibility demands stillness |
| 07 Dual CTA | DELIBERATE — staged entry | Two panels: fade-up with 150ms stagger left→right (600ms). CTA button: no animation, just hover state. | STILL — the decision moment is not decorated |

**Motion rhythm pattern:**
ACTIVE → CALM → RHYTHMIC → CINEMATIC → INVITING → FACTUAL → DELIBERATE

Each section's motion is DIFFERENT from the previous section. No two consecutive sections share the same motion character.

**The motion arc:** Motion intensity peaks at CINEMATIC (section 04), the featured project. This is appropriate — the most visually important section has the most sophisticated motion. The arc is: building (01→04) then releasing (04→07). Motion earns attention when it builds toward a peak and then subsides.

**Reduced motion behavior (all sections):**
All transforms collapse to `opacity: 0 → 1` transitions only. Duration: 200ms. The page remains fully usable and legible. No content is hidden or gated.

## 10. STORYTELLING RHYTHM

Each section tells a DIFFERENT story. The seven stories form a complete narrative arc.

| Section | Story Type | The Story Being Told | Narrative Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Hero | **World Story** | This is the brand's visual universe. You are now inside it. | Wordless (photography carries it) |
| 02 Brand Statement | **Belief Story** | This is what we think about surfaces, and why that matters. | First person, philosophical |
| 03 Collections | **Range Story** | This is what we make. We make more than one thing well. | Curatorial (names, not descriptions) |
| 04 Featured Project | **Achievement Story** | This is what we built. Here is who we built it with. | Third person, specific, factual |
| 05 Space Explorer | **Context Story** | This is where our surfaces live. Does your space belong here? | Second person, inviting |
| 06 Craft & Trust | **Process Story** | This is how we make what we make. Here is what that means. | First person, technical |
| 07 Dual CTA | **Direction Story** | Here is your next step. It depends on who you are. | Second person, direct |

**The storytelling arc:**
WORLD → BELIEF → RANGE → ACHIEVEMENT → CONTEXT → PROCESS → DIRECTION

Each story builds on the previous. BELIEF only resonates because the world has been established. RANGE only interests because the belief makes the range meaningful. ACHIEVEMENT only convinces because the range shows what is being proven. CONTEXT only engages because the achievement shows what is possible. PROCESS only reassures because the context has created stakes. DIRECTION only converts because all six previous stories have built the case.

**Rule:** No single section tells more than one story. The moment a section tries to tell two stories, it tells neither well.

## 11. WHERE TRUST IS BUILT

Trust is not built in one section. It is accumulated through the entire page.

### Layer 1 — Visual Trust (Section 01, Hero)
Photography quality communicates production quality. A brand with exceptional photography implies it has exceptional standards. This is the first and most immediate trust signal: the brand cares enough about its visual representation to invest in it.

### Layer 2 — Intellectual Trust (Section 02, Brand Statement)
A brand that writes specifically about materials — using real material terminology, taking an actual position rather than empty aspirational language — signals that it knows its subject. Generic marketing copy actively ERODES trust. Specific, considered copy builds it.

### Layer 3 — Aesthetic Trust (Section 03, Collections)
A curated editorial grid of 6 collections that are visually coherent, named with intention, and presented without discount energy communicates: this brand has taste. Taste is a form of trust — it implies consistent judgment.

### Layer 4 — Proof Trust (Section 04, Featured Project)
This is the most powerful trust layer. A named project — architect credited, location specified, date included — is a VERIFIABLE trust signal. The professional visitor can look up the project. The homeowner sees real-world results at real-world scale. Claims without proof are marketing. Projects ARE the proof.

### Layer 5 — Relevance Trust (Section 05, Space Explorer)
A brand that understands the visitor's specific context (their space, their project type) is a brand that has thought about its customers. Relevance is a trust signal: "they made this for someone like me."

### Layer 6 — Operational Trust (Section 06, Craft & Trust)
This is the final trust layer — the one that closes the gap between wanting and buying. ISO certification numbers. Years of production. Export market specifics. Factory location. These are operational trust signals: the brand can be AUDITED. A brand that provides verifiable operational credentials is a brand that has nothing to hide.

### The Trust Stack

```
Operational Trust  ███████████████████████████████ (Section 06)
Relevance Trust    ████████████████████            (Section 05)
Proof Trust        ██████████████████████████████  (Section 04)
Aesthetic Trust    █████████████████████           (Section 03)
Intellectual Trust ████████████████                (Section 02)
Visual Trust       ███████████████████████         (Section 01)
```

No single layer is sufficient alone. Together, they create a multi-dimensional trust profile that is very difficult for a visitor to consciously resist.

## 12. WHERE AUTHORITY IS BUILT

Authority is distinct from trust. Trust answers: "Can I rely on this brand?" Authority answers: "Does this brand have the right to speak about surfaces and architecture?"

### Authority Moment 1 — Brand Statement (Section 02)
A brand that makes a specific, considered claim about its materials — one that reveals genuine expertise rather than generic aspiration — asserts authority through knowledge. The authority here is INTELLECTUAL. The brand is not claiming to be good; it is demonstrating it knows what "good" means in this field.

### Authority Moment 2 — Collection Curation (Section 03)
Authority is also expressed through what a brand CHOOSES to show. A collections preview of 6 named, coherent, photographically distinct collections says: "we know what we are doing well enough to curate from our own range." Showing everything would be the opposite of authority.

### Authority Moment 3 — Named Architect Credit (Section 04)
This is the single most powerful authority signal on the page. When an architect with professional standing chooses a brand for a significant project, and when that brand is transparent enough to credit the architect by name, the authority transfer is complete. The architect's reputation becomes a co-signatory for the brand.

### Authority Moment 4 — Specific Credentials (Section 06)
ISO certification numbers (not just "we are certified"), specific export markets (not "we export globally"), specific production capabilities (not "high quality manufacturing"). Specificity is the language of authority. Vagueness is the language of insecurity.

## 13. WHERE PRODUCTS APPEAR

The homepage does not lead with products. Products arrive after context is established.

**First product appearance: Section 03 (Collections Preview)**
Products (collections) appear for the first time in section 03. At this point, the visitor has been given:
- The brand's world (section 01)
- The brand's beliefs (section 02)

They are now ready to evaluate products as brand objects rather than commodity tiles.

**Second product appearance: Section 04 (Featured Project)**
Collections are referenced as the material used in the featured project. This is contextual and supporting — the collection is named, linked, but not the focus. The space is the focus; the collection is what made it possible.

**Third product appearance: Section 05 (Space Explorer) — IMPLICIT**
Products are not explicitly shown in the space explorer, but each space tile leads to a Space Detail page where collections are curated by space type. The space explorer is the gateway, not the product display.

**Fourth product appearance: Section 07 (Dual CTA) — INDIRECT**
"Download Technical Catalog" references the full product range implicitly. It is a product touchpoint without product display.

**What the homepage does NOT do:**
- Feature product specifications
- Show product codes or dimensions
- Display price indicators
- Organize products by format or technical specification

All of these belong on the Collection Detail page, not the homepage. The homepage's product role is ASPIRATION and DISCOVERY — never specification.

---

## 14. WHERE PROJECTS APPEAR

**Primary project appearance: Section 04 (Featured Project)**
One project, presented at full narrative depth. This is the only place on the homepage where a project receives extended storytelling treatment. It occupies the most scroll-scroll distance of any section (200–280vh). It is the centerpiece of the homepage's credibility architecture.

**Secondary project reference: Section 03 (Collections Preview) — micro-reference**
Within each collection tile on the collections grid, a small indicator may note how many projects have used this collection ("3 Proje" / "3 Projects"). This is a subtle credibility signal that does not interrupt the editorial grid — it is metadata, not a headline.

**Project presence policy:**
Projects appear in one dedicated section and are referenced briefly in one supporting context. The homepage does not become a portfolio page — that is the Projects Index's role. The homepage uses one exceptional project to prove the brand's capability, then defers the full portfolio to a dedicated page.

## 15. FINAL CTA STRATEGY

### The Problem This Section Solves
By section 07, the visitor has been on a complete journey. They are not cold anymore. But they are TWO different types of visitors with two different needs:
- The professional architect or designer needs: access to technical resources → Professional Zone or Catalog
- The aspirational homeowner needs: sensory experience before commitment → Showroom Visit

One CTA cannot serve both without dilution. Two competing CTAs cannot both be primary. The solution is EXPLICIT AUDIENCE BIFURCATION — the only moment on the homepage where the two audiences are addressed separately.

### Structure of the Dual CTA Section

Two equal panels within a single dark section. Neither panel dominates. They sit at the same visual weight.

**Left Panel — For the professional:**
- Label (micro, non-eyebrow): A discipline or role reference
- Headline: What the professional wants to do
- Descriptor: One line. What this gets them.
- CTA: Ghost button. "Katalog İndir" or "Profesyonel Bölüm →"

**Right Panel — For the homeowner:**
- Label (micro, non-eyebrow): A desire or goal reference
- Headline: What the homeowner wants to experience
- Descriptor: One line. What this leads to.
- CTA: Ghost button. "Showroom Bul" or "Mekanları Keşfet →"

### Why Ghost Buttons (Not Filled) for Both

The primary CTA of the page ("Örnek İste" in the hero) uses the accent fill. Using filled buttons again in the dual CTA section would create visual competition with the hero CTA — two "primary" moments on the page. Ghost buttons acknowledge that these are SECONDARY actions — they are paths, not demands. They are respectful of the visitor's agency, which is consistent with luxury brand behavior.

### Why No "Request Samples" in This Section

The dual CTA deliberately does NOT repeat the sample request. That CTA was available in the hero (section 01) and on the collection detail page. By section 07, the visitor who wanted to request a sample has already done so. The visitor who is still in the page at section 07 needs a DIFFERENT type of action — either a deeper professional resource or a physical experience. Repeating the sample request here would signal that the brand doesn't trust the visitor to have seen it earlier.

### What Happens After Each Panel Click

**After "Katalog İndir" (Download Catalog):**
→ Opens a lightweight modal/overlay with catalog options (by collection, by effect) and a simple download form (Name, Email, Company). This keeps the visitor in the page experience rather than breaking to a new page. The modal closes and they land back on the homepage.

**After "Showroom Bul" (Find a Showroom):**
→ Navigates to the Contact page, anchored directly to the `#showroomlar` section. The visitor lands in the showroom listing without having to scroll past the sample request form first.

### Session-Level CTA Logic

The sample request CTA in the navigation persists throughout the session regardless of what section the visitor is viewing. The "Örnek İste" button in the hero provides an early conversion opportunity. The dual CTA provides the closing direction. Together, they bookend the journey without over-CTAing the middle sections.

**CTA-free zones:** Sections 02 (Brand Statement), 04 (Featured Project interior), and 06 (Craft & Trust) contain no CTA buttons. These sections do trust and credibility work — interrupting them with conversion asks would undermine their function.

## DECISION LOG REFERENCES

Key decisions made in this blueprint that were challenged and confirmed:

- Brand Statement precedes Collections (not follows) — because belief precedes evaluation in luxury psychology
- Featured Project is section 04 (not 03) — because product context must precede proof
- Space Explorer is section 05 (not 02) — because relevance is more powerful after aspiration and discovery
- Craft section has no CTA — because trust-building sections that sell are not trusted
- Dual CTA uses ghost buttons — because the accent fill primary action was already established in section 01
- Collections Preview uses an asymmetric grid of 6 (not 4 or 8) — editorial range without catalog excess
- Hero contains NO eyebrow label — the brand arrives without needing to announce itself
- Brand Statement is its own full section (not integrated into hero) — separate belief from impression
- No social media feed section — content quality dependency removed from launch
- No testimonials section — projects serve this function at higher credibility
